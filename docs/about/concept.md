# The idea

Most writing about AI in science covers large systems, such as autonomous "co-scientists" and agents that write papers.
This site covers daily research work: which tasks scientists give to agents, how they check the results, and what went wrong.

## Recipes

Each page is a recipe for one task.
It lists the steps, how to check the output, and the errors seen so far.
If a reusable skill exists for the task, the page links to it.
Recipes are written by people who use them.
A recipe that nobody has used yet is marked as a draft.

## One rule

Give agents the work whose output is quick and cheap to check.
[Delegate by verifiability](../guide/verifiability.md) explains the rule, and each recipe states how its output is checked.

## Where the first recipes come from

The first recipes come from a round-table of about 25 research group leaders at one computational research centre.
The centre wants to use agents in all parts of its research work, from choosing projects to administration.
It is not there yet, and some groups are further along than others.
The recipes record what worked, under which conditions, and what failed.

Names and details are removed from all examples.
The examples come from one centre and are based on what people reported.
People who already use agents a lot are overrepresented.
Other groups can test each recipe, add their own examples, and report where a recipe does not work for them.

## Relation to aiforscience.eu

[aiforscience.eu](https://aiforscience.eu) collects best practices for agentic AI in science, maintained by task forces and institutions.
Its practices state what should be done.
The recipes on this site show how to do it in a lab.
Each recipe links to the practices it follows, and practices can link to recipes as examples.

## Who it is for

- **Scientists** who use agents and want to use them for more tasks, safely.
- **Group leaders** who decide what their group should try next.
- **Core facilities and institutes** who decide which skills and systems to support.

## What institutes should build

Individual scientists start using agents without help.
Institutes need to provide what individuals cannot:

- Well-defined problems, each with a metric and several datasets.
- A shared library of reviewed skills.
- Sandboxed compute.
- Documentation that agents can read.
- Peer mentoring.
- Capable tools for administrative staff.
  Their processes set the pace for everyone else.
