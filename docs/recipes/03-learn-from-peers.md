## Summary

Agent use varies a lot, even within one lab.
General training courses often do not reach the people who use agents least.
What worked was peer learning: a round in which everyone shows what they actually do, followed by pairing the most experienced users with the least experienced.

## Steps

1. Hold a two-hour session where everyone shows how they use agents.
2. Pair your most experienced users with your least experienced.
3. Before expanding use, hold a session on what could go wrong.
4. Keep one shared skills repository, including skills that help people write new skills.
5. Hold an open drop-in meeting for several groups.

## How to check the output

After three months, ask again who uses agents every week, and for what.

## Examples

- In one group, about a fifth of members hardly used AI and about a fifth used it heavily.
- A first hands-on workshop on programming with agents was too fast for beginners, and they dropped out.
- Pairing experienced users with beginners increased use clearly.
- A later session on what could go wrong was rated among the most useful.
- Skills also cut costs: an agent that follows a tested skill uses far fewer tokens.

## Known problems

- Training designed by and for heavy users leaves beginners further behind.
- In the centre's survey, the most common obstacle was time to learn (53% of respondents).
